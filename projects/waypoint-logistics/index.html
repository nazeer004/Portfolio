03 / The design challenge

Make complex operations feel clear.

Logistics teams work across a constant flow of shipments, routes, vehicles, drivers and exceptions. When these details are scattered across separate views, it becomes harder to understand what needs attention first.

For this concept, I focused on a single operations workspace that surfaces the current state of the network and gives dispatchers a direct path into the records behind each status.

04 / UX decisions

Designed around the next action.

01

Start with network health

Shipment totals, delivery reliability and exceptions establish a quick operational picture before users move into detailed records.

02

Keep shipment discovery close

Search, status filters and a compact shipment table help users narrow the list without switching context.

03

Make details progressive

Selecting a shipment reveals its route, status, driver, vehicle and journey milestones without crowding the main overview.

04

Respect operational exceptions

Delayed shipments and activity events receive clear status treatment so they are easier to distinguish from routine movement.

05

Make common actions testable

The prototype includes shipment creation, filtering, detail views, global search and CSV export using browser-side sample data.

05 / Scope & boundaries

A working frontend concept.

The interface demonstrates responsive layouts and client-side interactions. It is not a production logistics system, and its sample records should not be interpreted as real operational performance.
